1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification defines the requirements for a technician marketplace platform that connects clients who need technical services with verified technicians who can provide those services. The platform will support discovery, job requests, technician matching, quotations, booking, communication, payment, service completion, reviews, verification, dispute management and administration.
1.2 Objectives
Provide clients with a reliable way to discover and engage qualified technicians.
Increase trust through structured technician identity, professional and business verification.
Give technicians a digital workspace for managing jobs, quotations, schedules, customers and earnings.
Provide transparent workflows from service request through completion and review.
Create a scalable marketplace foundation that can support multiple service categories and geographic areas.
Provide administrators with tools for marketplace governance, verification, disputes, payments and analytics.
1.3 Definitions
Term	Definition
Client	A user who requests, books or pays for technician services.
Technician	A professional or service provider offering technical services through the platform.
Job	A service request and its associated lifecycle, communications, documents and transactions.
Quotation	A technician's proposed price and scope for a requested job.
Verification	A process used to establish the authenticity of technician identity, credentials or business information.
Marketplace	The platform connecting clients and technicians.
Dispute	A formal issue raised by a client or technician concerning a job, payment or service.
Service Area	The geographic area in which a technician agrees to provide services.
2. Product Vision and Background
The platform will function as a trusted digital marketplace for technical services. Rather than acting only as a technician directory, it will manage the complete service lifecycle: discovery or job posting, matching, quotation, booking, communication, service execution, payment, review and post-service support.
2.1 Core Value Proposition
For clients: access to discoverable, verified and reviewable technicians with clear service information.
For technicians: access to customers plus tools for operating their service business digitally.
For administrators: centralized control over users, verification, marketplace quality, payments, disputes and platform performance.

4. User Personas

A person or organization seeking technical assistance. The client values trust, availability, transparent pricing, convenient scheduling, communication and service accountability.
4.2 Technician
An individual professional or technical service business seeking customers and tools to manage service operations. The technician values qualified leads, predictable workflows, reputation, earnings visibility and efficient scheduling.
4.3 Administrator
An authorized platform operator responsible for maintaining marketplace integrity, verifying technicians, managing disputes, monitoring transactions, moderating content and reviewing analytics.

6. Functional Requirements
6.1 Authentication and Accounts
FR-001: The system shall allow clients to create accounts.
FR-002: The system shall allow technicians to create accounts and submit professional profiles.
FR-003: The system shall authenticate users securely.
FR-004: The system shall support password recovery and account recovery.
FR-005: The system shall enforce role-based permissions.
FR-006: The system shall allow users to update permitted profile information.
FR-007: The system shall support account deactivation subject to platform rules.
6.2 Technician Profiles
FR-010: Technicians shall be able to provide a profile photo or business logo.
FR-011: Technicians shall provide service categories and skills.
FR-012: Technicians shall provide experience information.
FR-013: Technicians shall provide service areas.
FR-014: Technicians shall configure working hours and availability.
FR-015: Technicians shall optionally provide certifications, licenses and business information.
FR-016: The system shall display verification status and verified attributes.
FR-017: The system shall display relevant reviews and completed-job statistics.
6.3 Technician Verification
FR-020: The system shall support identity verification.
FR-021: The system shall support phone and email verification.
FR-022: The system shall support business verification where applicable.
FR-023: The system shall support professional credential verification where applicable.
FR-024: Administrators shall approve, reject or request additional verification information.
FR-025: The system shall record verification dates, status and expiry where applicable.
FR-026: The system shall prevent unauthorized changes to verified information without re-verification.
6.4 Client Search and Discovery
FR-030: Clients shall search by service category and skill.
FR-031: Clients shall filter technicians by service area, availability, price range, rating and verification attributes.
FR-032: Clients shall view technician profiles.
FR-033: Clients shall save technicians as favourites.
FR-034: The system shall support location-aware service-area matching.
6.5 Job Posting
FR-040: Clients shall create service requests.
FR-041: Clients shall describe the problem or required service.
FR-042: Clients shall upload supporting photographs or documents.
FR-043: Clients shall specify preferred date/time and urgency.
FR-044: Clients may specify an indicative budget.
FR-045: The system shall generate a unique job identifier.
6.6 Matching
FR-050: The system shall identify technicians whose skills match the requested service.
FR-051: The system shall consider service area.
FR-052: The system shall consider technician availability.
FR-053: The system may consider experience, verification, performance history and client preferences.
FR-054: Matching factors shall be configurable without changing core application code where practical.
6.7 Quotations
FR-060: Technicians shall submit quotations for eligible jobs.
FR-061: Quotations shall support labour, materials, transport, taxes/fees and discounts.
FR-062: Quotations shall contain a total amount.
FR-063: Quotations shall have an expiry date/time.
FR-064: Clients shall accept or decline quotations.
FR-065: Accepted quotations shall be linked to the relevant booking/job.
6.8 Booking and Scheduling
FR-070: Clients shall schedule service appointments.
FR-071: Technicians shall maintain availability.
FR-072: The system shall detect scheduling conflicts.
FR-073: Clients and technicians shall be able to reschedule subject to applicable rules.
FR-074: The system shall record appointment history.
6.9 Job Lifecycle
The primary job lifecycle shall support the following states:
REQUESTED → QUOTED → ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED → ASSIGNED → EN_ROUTE → ARRIVED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED → PAYMENT → REVIEWED
FR-080: Authorized users shall see the current job status.
FR-081: Status changes shall be recorded in an audit history.
FR-082: The system shall restrict invalid state transitions.
FR-083: Completion shall capture appropriate completion evidence where required.
6.10 Messaging
FR-090: Clients and technicians shall communicate through job-specific conversations.
FR-091: Users shall be able to share permitted images and documents.
FR-092: The system shall preserve message timestamps and participants.
FR-093: Administrators shall have controlled access to information required for moderation, disputes and safety.
6.11 Payments
FR-100: The system shall record payment requests and transaction status.
FR-101: The system shall integrate with supported payment providers.
FR-102: The system shall record platform fees and technician earnings.
FR-103: The system shall support refunds according to platform rules and provider capabilities.
FR-104: The system shall maintain transaction history and receipts.
6.12 Reviews and Reputation
FR-110: Eligible clients shall be able to rate completed services.
FR-111: Reviews shall be associated with completed jobs.
FR-112: The system shall support structured rating dimensions such as quality, punctuality, professionalism and value.
FR-113: Users shall be able to report inappropriate reviews.
FR-114: Administrators shall moderate reported reviews.
6.13 Disputes and Support
FR-120: Clients and technicians shall be able to open disputes.
FR-121: A dispute shall contain a reason, description and relevant evidence.
FR-122: Administrators shall review and update dispute status.
FR-123: Disputes shall have an audit trail.
FR-124: Resolutions may include payment adjustments, refunds, closure or other permitted actions.
6.14 Warranty
FR-130: Technicians may define a service warranty for eligible jobs.
FR-131: The system shall record warranty start and end dates.
FR-132: Clients may initiate warranty claims during the active warranty period.
FR-133: Warranty claims shall be linked to the original job.
6.15 Technician Business Workspace
FR-140: Technicians shall have a dashboard for incoming and active jobs.
FR-141: Technicians shall manage quotations and appointments.
FR-142: Technicians shall view earnings and payout history.
FR-143: Technicians shall view customer/job history.
FR-144: The platform may provide invoice and expense management in later releases.
7. System Workflows
7.1 Client Direct Booking Workflow
1.Client searches for a service.
2.Client filters and views technician profiles.
3.Client selects a technician.
4.Client submits a service request.
5.Technician accepts the request or submits a quotation.
6.Client accepts the quotation.
7.Appointment is scheduled.
8.Technician performs the service.
9.Job is marked completed.
10.Payment is processed.
11.Client submits a review.
7.2 Job Marketplace Workflow
12.Client posts a job.
13.System classifies the request and identifies matching technicians.
14.Eligible technicians receive the request.
15.Technicians submit quotations or express interest.
16.Client reviews available proposals.
17.Client selects a technician.
18.Booking and payment arrangements are established.
19.Service is performed.
20.Job is completed and reviewed.

11. Security and Privacy
Use secure authentication and password hashing through a trusted authentication mechanism.
Implement role-based access control and resource-level authorization.
Encrypt sensitive data in transit and use appropriate protection for sensitive data at rest.
Protect uploaded identity and credential documents from unauthorized access.
Apply rate limiting and abuse controls to authentication and public endpoints.
Maintain security and administrative audit logs.
Minimize collection of personal data to what is required for platform functions.
Provide appropriate privacy notices, consent mechanisms and data-management processes.
Separate administrative privileges using least-privilege principles.

13. Payments and Financial Requirements
Payment functionality shall be implemented through compliant payment providers appropriate to the target market. The platform shall separate payment authorization, transaction recording, platform fees, refunds and technician payouts.
Payment methods should be configurable by deployment/market.
The system shall not store raw card data unless the chosen architecture and compliance obligations explicitly support it.
Every financial transaction shall have a unique identifier.
Payment and payout statuses shall be synchronized with the provider where supported.
Refunds shall be traceable to the original transaction.
Commission and fee calculations shall be configurable.

17. Technical and Architectural Requirements
17.1 High-Level Architecture
The system should follow a modular web application architecture consisting of client applications, backend services, a relational database, object/file storage, notification services and external integrations.
Presentation Layer: responsive web/mobile-friendly interfaces for clients, technicians and administrators.
Application Layer: authentication, authorization, job management, matching, booking, payments, reviews, disputes and notifications.
Data Layer: relational database for transactional records and object storage for files.
Integration Layer: payment, maps, messaging, notification and verification providers.
Observability Layer: logging, monitoring, metrics and audit records.
17.2 Suggested Technology Direction
Frontend: React + TypeScript with a component-based UI architecture.
Backend: a structured REST/HTTP API or equivalent service architecture.
Database: PostgreSQL or another production-grade relational database.
File storage: object storage with private access controls.
Authentication: managed or self-hosted secure identity service.
Deployment: cloud-hosted architecture capable of horizontal scaling as demand increases.
The final technology stack should be selected after detailed technical design, deployment, budget and target-market requirements are established.

20. Acceptance Criteria
Area	Acceptance Criterion

21. Risks and Assumptions
21.1 Risks
Fraudulent technician identities or credentials.
Poor service quality despite verification.
Payment fraud and chargebacks.
Disputes over job scope or quotation changes.
Privacy risks involving identity documents and location information.
Marketplace imbalance between technician supply and client demand.
Operational complexity of refunds, warranties and disputes.
Dependence on third-party payment, mapping and messaging providers.
21.2 Assumptions
The platform will initially operate in a defined target market and service area.
Applicable legal, payment, consumer-protection and data-protection requirements will be assessed before production deployment.
Technician verification requirements will vary according to service category and jurisdiction.
The first release will prioritize web access and a complete service lifecycle.
